# Shoulder Instability / Dislocation

## What is Shoulder Instability / Dislocation?

It's when the shoulder has too much movement or poor control, causing:

- The joint to partially slip (instability)
- Or fully come out (dislocation)

**Key idea:** The shoulder is too loose or loses control, instead of being "stuck" like frozen shoulder.

---

## The 2 Main Types (Conditions)

### 1) Atraumatic Instability

**What it is:**

- No major injury
- Caused by loose connective tissue (laxity)

**Can be:**

- Unidirectional (one direction)
- Multidirectional (multiple directions)

**Problem:**

- Poor muscle control
- Joint moves too much → instability

**Exercises to fix it (MAIN focus):**

- Rotator cuff strengthening
- Scapular stabilization
- Proprioception/control exercises (teach joint control)
- Closed-chain exercises (wall push-ups, weight shifts)

**Key point:** Rehab = strengthening and control

### 2) Traumatic Instability (Dislocation)

**What it is:**

- Caused by a specific injury or high-force event
- Shoulder dislocates (comes out of joint)

**Common Mechanism of Injury:**

- Arm in:
  - Elevation
  - External rotation
  - Horizontal abduction

**Most common:**

- Anterior dislocation (front of shoulder)

---

## Clinical Presentation (What You See)

- Pain (especially with external rotation at 90° abduction)
- Limited overhead movement
- Feeling of instability
- Possible axillary nerve injury

---

## Immobilization (Important!)

- **Young, active patients:** 6–8 weeks
  - Tissue is more flexible → takes longer to tighten
- **Older, less active patients:** ~2 weeks
  - Tissue is stiffer → tightens faster

---

## Rehab Focus (After Immobilization)

### Restore Motion (carefully)

- Gradual ROM exercises
- Avoid positions that caused dislocation early on

### Strengthen & Stabilize

- Rotator cuff strengthening
- Scapular muscles
- Dynamic stability exercises

### Improve Control

- Proprioception training (joint awareness)
- Functional movement training

---

## Easy Way to Remember

- **Atraumatic** = loose from the start
  - No injury
  - Fix with strength & control
- **Traumatic** = caused by injury
  - Dislocation event
  - Needs protection → then rehab

---

## Super Simple Summary

Shoulder instability/dislocation is when the joint has too much movement or loses control:

- **Atraumatic:** loose joint → strengthen muscles
- **Traumatic:** injury causes dislocation → immobilize, then rehab

- Most common dislocation = anterior
- Caused by: ER + abduction + elevation
