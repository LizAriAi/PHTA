# Shoulder Labrum Injuries

## Bankart Lesion

- **What it is:** Tear of the anterior-inferior glenoid labrum
- **Usually happens with:** Anterior shoulder dislocation
- **Repair:** Bankart repair = labrum is reattached (often with anchors/staples)

## SLAP Lesion

- **What it is:** Tear of the superior labrum, goes front (anterior) to back (posterior)
- **Affects:** Biceps anchor (important for overhead stability)

## Hill-Sachs Lesion

- **What it is:** Compression fracture on the posterolateral humeral head
- **How it happens:** During anterior dislocation — humeral head hits the front rim of the glenoid

## Rehab Considerations — Anterior Shoulder Dislocation

### Acute Phase

**Main goal:** Protect healing tissue

**Avoid:**
- External rotation + abduction (especially at 90°+)
- Hyperextension

**Safe positions:**
- Arm at side
- Scapular plane (scaption)

**Exercises:**
- Isometrics only:
  - Rotator cuff (especially subscapularis)
  - Deltoid
  - Long head of biceps
- Very light contraction, no movement

### Subacute / Chronic Phase

**Goals:**
- Restore motion
- Improve stability
- Strengthen rotator cuff

**Stretching:**
- Posterior capsule:
  - Cross-body stretch
  - Sleeper stretch

**Strengthening:**
- Internal rotation
- Horizontal adduction
- External rotation (carefully)

**External Rotation Rule:**
- Avoid dislocation position early
- Limit ER to ~0–50° in subacute stage

**Later Chronic Stage:**
- Gradual return to:
  - Full ER
  - ER in abduction position
  - Closed-chain stability work (very important)

## External Rotator Exercise Progression

### 🟢 Acute (1 week)

- **Contraction:** Isometric only
- **Arm position:** Arm at side (0° abduction)
- **ROM:** No movement
- **Resistance:** Very light manual resistance or towel push into wall
- **Example:** "Push outward into therapist's hand without moving arm"

### 🟡 Early Subacute (3 weeks)

- **Contraction:** Isometric → light active-assisted
- **Arm position:** Still at side or slight scaption
- **ROM:** Very small (0–15° ER max)
- **Resistance:** Light resistance band OR manual resistance
- **Example:** Band external rotation with elbow tucked at side

### 🟠 Late Subacute (6 weeks)

- **Contraction:** Active concentric + eccentric
- **Arm position:** Scapular plane (30–45° abduction)
- **ROM:** Moderate ROM (up to ~50° ER)
- **Resistance:** Light → moderate band resistance
- **Example:** Side-lying external rotation with light dumbbell

### 🔵 Chronic (12+ weeks)

- **Contraction:** Full dynamic strengthening (concentric + eccentric + endurance)
- **Arm position:** 90° abduction (eventually return to sport positions)
- **ROM:** Full ROM
- **Resistance:** Moderate → heavy resistance
- **Functional / sport-specific load**
- **Example:** 90/90 external rotation with cable or band

---

## Simple Summary

- **Acute:** Protect → isometrics only
- **3 weeks:** Light controlled motion
- **6 weeks:** Strengthen in safer range
- **12+ weeks:** Full functional strengthening
