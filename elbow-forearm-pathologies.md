# Elbow & Forearm Pathology Notes

---

## Lateral Elbow Tendinopathy (Tennis Elbow)

- **Tendon affected:** Extensor Carpi Radialis Brevis (ECRB)
- **Main symptom:** Lateral elbow pain with wrist extension, gripping, lifting

### Rehab Phases & Stretching

| Phase | When | What's Done |
|-------|------|-------------|
| **Acute** | First ~1-2 weeks | Rest, ice, NSAIDs — **NO stretching** |
| **Subacute** | ~2-3 weeks | Gentle stretching begins, isometric loading |
| **Remodeling** | ~3-6 weeks | Progressive loading, **full stretching** allowed |
| **Return to Function** | ~6+ weeks | Sport/activity-specific strengthening |

**Key point:** You can **fully stretch wrist extensors** once you're in the **remodeling phase** (after acute inflammation settles, ~3+ weeks in). Start gentle in subacute, full stretch in remodeling.

## Cubital Tunnel Syndrome

- **Location:** Medial elbow, between medial epicondyle and olecranon
- The **cubital tunnel** houses the **ulnar nerve** (the "funny bone" spot)
- **Symptoms:** Numbness/tingling in ring & pinky finger, weakness in grip
- Often worsened by: Prolonged elbow flexion, leaning on elbow

---

## Medial Elbow Tendinopathy (Golfer's Elbow)

- **Tendon affected:** Flexor Carpi Radialis / Pronator Teres (common flexor origin)
- **Location:** Medial elbow
- **Main symptom:** Medial elbow pain with wrist flexion, gripping, pronation

---

## Volkmann's Ischemic Contracture (Compartment Syndrome)

**5 P's of Compartment Syndrome:**

| P | Meaning |
|---|---------|
| **Pain** | Severe, disproportionate to injury |
| **Pallor** | Pale/white skin |
| **Pulselessness** | No pulse distal to injury |
| **Paresthesia** | Numbness/tingling |
| **Paralysis** | Loss of motor function (late sign!) |

- Caused by: Increased pressure in muscle compartment → cuts off blood flow
- Emergency! Needs immediate treatment
