# Scapulohumeral Rhythm

## The 2:1 Ratio

- **Total shoulder flexion/abduction:** 180°
- **Glenohumeral joint:** 120°
- **Scapulothoracic (scapula):** 60°

## Scapula Movement Pattern

- Moves the **whole time** during arm elevation
- **Minimal at first** (0–30°) — scapulohumeral rhythm starts gradually
- **More obvious after 30°** — scapula starts rotating significantly

## WHY This Matters

- Prevents impingement
- Keeps joint stable
- Allows full ROM

# Force Couples

## Upward Rotation Force Couple

**Purpose:** Move scapula upward (lifting arm overhead)

**Muscles:**
- Upper trapezius → pulls up
- Lower trapezius → pulls down
- Serratus anterior → pulls forward

**WHY it works:** Together they produce smooth upward rotation — Upper Trap + Lower Trap pull opposite directions but both contribute to upward rotation when Serratus Anterior adds the forward pull.

---

## Glenohumeral Joint Stability Force Couple

**Purpose:** Keep humeral head centered in glenoid fossa

**Muscles:**
- Deltoid (especially middle fibers) → pulls humerus **up**
- Rotator cuff → pulls humerus **down** + stabilizes
  - Supraspinatus
  - Infraspinatus
  - Teres minor
  - Subscapularis

**WHY:** Deltoid pulls superiorly (causes impingement at acromion), rotator cuff counters this by pulling inferiorly. Prevents humeral head from translating upward.

# Ligaments (Basic Function)

## Glenohumeral (GH) Joint

| Ligament | Function |
|----------|----------|
| **Superior GH** | Limits inferior translation |
| **Middle GH** | Limits anterior movement |
| **Inferior GH** ⭐ | MOST IMPORTANT — stabilizes in abduction + ER (dislocation position) |

## Elbow Joint

| Ligament | Function |
|----------|----------|
| **Ulnar Collateral (UCL)** | Resists valgus stress (medial elbow) |
| **Radial Collateral (RCL)** | Resists varus stress (lateral elbow) |

## Wrist Joint

| Ligaments | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Palmar ligaments** | Stronger, stabilize wrist in flexion |
| **Dorsal ligaments** | Weaker |

**WHY:** Wrist more stable in flexion than extension (anatomical position favors flexion stability).

# Forearm Motions & Prime Movers

## Pronation

- **Prime movers:** Pronator Teres, Pronator Quadratus

**WHY:** Both cross anteriorly → pull radius over ulna (forearm turns palm down)

## Supination

- **Prime movers:** Biceps Brachii ⭐ (strongest), Supinator

**WHY:** Both wrap around radius → rotate it back to anatomical position (forearm turns palm up)

---

# Elbow MMT

## Elbow Flexion
- **Position:** Test at ~90° flexion
- **Resistance:** Resist further flexion

## Elbow Extension
- **Position:** Start flexed
- **Resistance:** Resist extension

## Gravity Minimized
- Perform in **horizontal plane** (sitting, arm supported on table)

---

# Power vs Precision Grips

## Power Grip
- Whole hand involved
- Example: holding a hammer

## Precision Grip
- Fingers only (fine motor control)
- Example: holding a pencil

---

# Key Innervations

| Nerve | Innervates |
|-------|------------|
| **Musculocutaneous** | Elbow flexors (biceps, brachialis) |
| **Radial** | Elbow extensors (triceps), wrist extensors |
| **Median** | Most forearm flexors, thenar muscles |
| **Ulnar** | Intrinsic hand muscles, flexor carpi ulnaris |
