# Shoulder & Elbow Pathologies - Study Guide

## I. Adhesive Capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder)

Involves synovial inflammation and fibrosis of the joint capsule, significantly reducing the joint cavity.

### Mechanisms
- **Primary**: Idiopathic (unknown cause), most common in females aged 40–65
- **Secondary**: Triggered by trauma, immobilization, diabetes, thyroid issues, or chronic tendinitis

### The Four Stages

| Stage | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Pre-Adhesive** | Gradual onset of pain, worse at night/with movement; loss of external rotation (ER) ROM begins |
| **Freezing** | Intense pain even at rest; ROM decreases in all directions |
| **Frozen** | "Stiff" stage; pain mainly at end ranges; GH muscles may atrophy; ER is the most restricted motion (Capsular Pattern) |
| **Thawing** | Recovery stage; pain decreases and ROM begins to increase |

### Rehab Focus
- **Freezing** stage: Focus on ROM and isometrics
- **Frozen/Thawing** stage: Focus on correcting mechanics, joint mobilization, and gradual resistance

## II. Subacromial Impingement & Rotator Cuff (RTC) Tears

Impingement = mechanical compression of soft tissue (supraspinatus tendon or bursa) in the subacromial space.

### Clinical Presentation
- Painful arc between 60–120 degrees of abduction
- Pain with overhead activities
- Difficulty lifting

### Rehab Considerations

**Acute:**
- Pain relief
- Postural awareness
- Avoiding impingement positions

**Subacute/Chronic:**
- Strengthen "weak" muscles: ERs, Serratus Anterior, Lower Trap
- Stretch "tight" ones: Pectorals, Internal Rotators

### RTC Repairs
- Most re-tears occur in the first 4 months
- Patients must follow strict protocols, often starting with PROM only

## III. Glenohumeral Instability & Dislocations

### Anterior Dislocation
- Most common type
- Usually caused by a force when the arm is in elevation, ER, and horizontal abduction

### Key Lesions
- **Bankart**: Tear of the anterior-inferior glenoid labrum
- **Hill-Sachs**: Compression fracture of the posterolateral humeral head

### Contraindications
- Initially AVOID combining ER and Abduction at 90° or greater

## IV. Elbow Pathologies

### Lateral Elbow Tendinopathy (Tennis Elbow)
- Overuse of common extensor tendon (primarily ECRB)
- Pain with resisted wrist extension or passive wrist flexion

### Medial Elbow Tendinopathy (Golfer's Elbow)
- Overuse of common flexer tendon
- Pain with resisted wrist flexion or passive wrist extension

### Ulnar Collateral Ligament (UCL)
- Often injured in overhead throwing athletes due to valgus stress
- Rehab focuses on regaining extension and avoiding valgus stress
- **No ER exercises early on**

### Fractures

**Olecranon:**
- No flexion past 90° for first 8 weeks
- No active resistance extension for first 8 weeks

**Volkmann's Ischemic Contracture**
- Serious vascular complication after fracture
- Watch for the "5 P's": Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis

## V. Lab & Exercise Highlights

### Self-Stretches
- **Table stretches**: Flexion, ER, Abduction
- **Sleeper stretch**: For IR
- **Doorway stretches**: For ER

### Stabilization
- Scapular retraction (prone or standing with theraband)
- Scapular protraction (supine or theraband)
- "Superman" (Y and I) for upward rotation

### Resistance
- Scaption (full can)
- "Hold-up" position for ER
- Side-lying ER with free weights
